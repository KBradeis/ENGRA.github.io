Engineering / Project 04 · Assistive tech

Prosthetic Hand

Printed and assembled an open-source prosthetic hand, tested its grip, then scaled it up for larger users.

Partner

Ben

Timeline

Spring 2026 · ENGR 11A

Tools

3D printing · open-source files

Focus

Assistive technology

Process

How it came together

Step 01

This is our main print being tested to see how much tension it can handle. This has been an interesting project since it can be customized to whatever someone might need which makes it very interesting. I think that I’m very enthusiastic about the idea of making something that would significantly make someone’s life easier.

Step 02

Figuring out how to access all the files wasn’t challenging for me since I knew what the final product would look like and what I wanted to do. I was able to easily navigate the website while looking at the tutorials and videos. I think this is very helpful to beginners who are starting out. If I were to make the website I’d try to use more simple language everywhere and make sure that the important steps like devices and community are in different tabs.

Step 03

Me and my partner Ben have realised that the hand requires a significant amount for you to hold onto something. This is something that me and my partner want to try and work on so it is no longer a strenuous thing for users. We might do that by lengthening the wrist compartment. We also wanted to make the hand compartment for people with above average hand sizes.

Contributing back

The most challenging part of the documentation to follow was looking at the STL files since all of them just seem to be randomly organized when I downloaded them onto my drive. I would organize these pieces in groups of how they should be printed for the user if they want to follow that way. Another thing that I thought was hard to navigate was working with the whipple tree and having to put the string through the entire hand. Ben and I had people trying to use our method of using a solder to put things inside the hand. Making it easier for everyone.

Step 04

For our partner modification we made the hand bigger so that people with bigger than average upper limb difference. Or if you needed a bigger hand to do something than that exists.

Reflection

I think that Open Source AT is important because it gives people the opportunity to help one another and improve any designs. This gives the people with disabilities access to the direct source and make any modifications that they see are fit. It also makes people able to get the tools that they need for their day to day life.

Bridge to capstone

This project helped me learn that there is an unmet need of people with disabilities that don’t have access to your everyday things that people will never normally think of. My group and I are working on gaming prosthetics for people with maybe upper limb disabilities and starting with this project was very helpful. This is something that I never really thought was important but then I saw some that said technology isn’t being used properly if people with disabilities can’t enjoy it.
