Engineering / Project 05 · Capstone

Assistive Gaming Device

How can we make quick, complex game inputs possible, and affordable, for players with upper limb differences? Our team designed a 3D printed mouse handpiece with foot-pedal clicks and iterated on it four times.

01 · The problem

Fast games are built for two hands.

An estimated 33 million+ gamers in the US have a disability. Most accessible controllers focus on bigger buttons and easier switches. That helps in slower games, but it holds players back in competitive ones, where top players make up to 10 actions per second. The player’s decisions can be lightning-fast; the hardware is the bottleneck.

“How can we make a cost-effective system that makes quick, complex inputs possible for players with upper limb differences?”

02 · Research

Designing for Duncan.

User persona

Duncan, high-school junior

Plays competitive shooters with friends every night. He has an upper limb difference and spends his time hunting for custom keybinds and modified gear to keep up.

33M+

gamers with disabilities in the US

We also printed and tested existing open-source solutions, including Joy-Con trigger extenders and a one-handed controller attachment, to learn which mechanisms worked and where they fell short.

03 · Design principles

What success had to mean.

A

Hands-free clicks

Left and right clicks move to foot switches, so the upper limb only steers.

B

Wires out of the way

Cable management can’t get between the player and the game.

C

Survives real use

Passes desk-height drop tests and load tests without breaking.

04 · Prototyping

Four cycles, one change at a time.

Cycle 1 · Baseline

First PLA prototype

A working setup, but a handful of loose wires came out of the footmount.

Cycle 2 · CAD change

Routed the wires inside

We hollowed the footmount and added internal wire channels. This made the biggest difference in usability.

Cycle 3 · CAM change

Tuned the slicer

We changed print orientation and slicer settings to get cleaner, stronger parts.

Cycle 4 · Material change

Switched PLA to PETG

The PLA mouse housing cracked at the clips. The same design printed in PETG was tougher and held up.

05 · Results

Tougher than it looks.

500 N

top-down load on the handpiece with no break

Stairs

survived a stair drop test

2 pedals

left and right clicks, fully hands-free

06 · Reflection

From consumer to creator.

At the start, I didn’t believe we could build this. Working from a rough first prototype to something genuinely useful changed how I approach making things: start from what the user needs, and let testing decide the next change. The CAD change made the biggest difference; the material change taught us the most, because a good design can still fail in the wrong material.

07 · Build guide

Make your own.

Print files

Handpiece v3 · Footmount v3

Prusa MK4 · 0.4 mm nozzle · 0.20 mm layers · 15% infill. Handpiece: about 2 h 49 min and 104 g. Footmount: about 3 h 4 min and 141 g. The mouse bottom is the open-source Bambu design.
