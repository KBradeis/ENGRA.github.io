TimeWise

A planning app for college students whose days don’t go as planned. I built it for a class project with one brief: use Claude to solve a problem I care about, then get people to actually use it.

01 · The problem

One thing runs late, and the whole day slides.

Most students don’t need a stricter schedule. They need help when the schedule stops matching the day. A lecture runs over, the problem set starts late and eats lunch, a café shift can’t move, the gym gets skipped, and the essay never happens. Tomorrow already looks full.

02 · The product

What TimeWise does about it.

Today

See the day at a glance

What’s happening now, what’s next, and how much free time is left. Overloaded days get flagged early.

Replan

Fix the rest of the day

Say how late you’re running. Classes and shifts stay put, everything else shifts, and what won’t fit moves to tomorrow.

Reflect

Look back without guilt

Mark what happened to each block, then get one pattern and one small change for tomorrow.

Connect

Use your calendar

Read-only Google Calendar and Notion connections, or import a file from Apple Calendar, Outlook or Moodle.

03 · Gaining traction

Built to learn from real users.

TimeWise is free and takes about five minutes to set up, so students can try it on their own week. An early-access form asks three quick questions about how people plan now, their biggest time struggle and whether they’d use it weekly, and those answers decide what gets built next. The site also counts which demo features get used, anonymously.
